I do my best work when a team is trying to figure out what actually matters before they spend months building the wrong thing.

I'm a product strategist and UX researcher with 15+ years helping leadership teams turn unclear product bets into workflows people can actually use. My background is healthcare and SaaS, with consulting work across early-stage products, enterprise platforms, and clinical environments.

My work usually starts where the official process stops explaining what is happening. A tool launches, training happens, usage stalls, and the organization starts arguing from opinion. I go into the workflow, separate evidence from assumption, and bring the decision back to what users, operators, and the business all need to be true.

I'm finishing an MBA at Rice University in Innovation and Entrepreneurship. I keep choosing problems in healthcare, wellness, and AI-enabled products because adoption has consequences there. AI can clear noise and expose patterns people miss, but judgment still has to come from someone who understands the work. Healthcare keeps pulling me back because the people with the least power over the product are often the ones who feel the friction most: patients trying to find trials, nurses using software they cannot opt out of, and technologists managing scanner queues through brittle tools. Those are the people I keep designing for.

Why

Point of View

The hard part is not knowing what the technology can do. The hard part is changing how people work without losing sight of the people doing the work.

That is where I spend most of my time. Research turns up something the plan missed. An assumption stops holding. The quiet user group, the one nobody centered in the roadmap, turns out to be carrying the workflow. At that point, the team has a choice: update the plan, or keep shipping what already got approved. I stay in the room for that conversation.

AI is useful when it understands the room: customer behavior, operational constraints, regulatory pressure, and the expertise already inside the team. That context has to stay alive after launch. Otherwise the useful signal gets trapped in the discovery phase, where it cannot help anyone.
